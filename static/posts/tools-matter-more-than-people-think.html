Tools matter more than people think

As most noobs do, I looked at the programmers I respected and, for some reason, held on to the deep, irrational idea that to become as skilled as them, I had to use their tools. I know it's completely absurd to think like this. Programming is, despite what some might think, an intellectual job, so most of the time it doesn't matter if you use VS Code or a text editor to write your programs. As long as you're intelligent and fast at converting your thoughts into code, the tools you use for that are nothing but meaningless.

It took me a long time to come to this simple conclusion. But in that relentless process of trying to copy other people's configs and flows, I found that, despite the tools not mattering, you should still take some time to go down the rabbit hole of configuring your tools. Why? First and most importantly, because it's fun. Second, because it will make you spend more time learning about computers and software, especially the software you use. And that's the important part, the part that makes you faster at translating ideas into code. It's not your editor, it's the knowledge you have of it.

I use Neovim, by the way. I started because I thought that to become as great a programmer as the ones I respected, I had to learn vim keybindings and code in the terminal. So I went all in on Neovim. Did I become as great as them? Not even close. But I did become a much better programmer overall.

So if you're thinking about going into this rabbit hole, do it. Not for the promise that you'll become a 10x engineer by using tool X instead of tool Y, but because in the process you will learn, and learning is what makes you a 10x engineer.
