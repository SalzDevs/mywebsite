Calculators are not AGI

I was born in 2002. For most OGs of the internet, that makes me a complete noob — I wasn't there at the beginning. By the time I showed up, the internet was already an established, everyday thing, even in the small European country I'm from.

But there's one thing I can say for certain: I lived through the stupidest era of internet discussion. On one side you have the people who believe AI has little to no value, and on the other, the people who believe the world will end next week because the next Anthropic or OpenAI release is the first version of Ultron. As a mediocre programmer myself, I see a lot of use and benefit in AI. But being a mediocre programmer is also exactly why I can understand people who claim that really high-quality code can't be written by a San Francisco finance bro who doesn't even know what a pointer is.

What frustrates me most in all of this is that people are starting to believe learning to code is a thing of the past — something you shouldn't do anymore. Even if we reached AGI next week (and I don't mean an LLM that can write okay code by itself), learning to code would still be a great thing to pursue. Not because it will make you rich. Not because it will make you super smart, or because it translates to other fields of your life. But because it might be fun.

Do people stop playing chess because Stockfish exists? Do people stop playing table tennis because of those undefeatable robotic arms? The answer is a resounding no — and the reason is that it's fun. Most people are trying to optimize their lives into becoming the smartest and the greatest at something, but I promise you: if that's what you want, you just have to find the thing you have fun doing.

Perseverance is, and will always be, part of the skill set you need to become great at something. There will always be days when the couch feels warmer and softer than ever, but I promise you, you will always have a better chance of getting there when you love the process. That's why I believe the most successful people are not the smartest ones, but the ones who — despite what everyone said — went and followed the things that made "work" enjoyable.

So my final take is this: even if you think AI will replace programming, or whatever else you feel an eagerness to try, try it anyway. Worst case, you lose some time. Best case, you find something you'd do even if money weren't the goal — and it shouldn't be. Don't become a sellout, please!
