Judo is just falling with intention

Twenty-five years in, the thing I still use most from judo is not a throw. It's knowing how to land.

The first thing you learn on the tatami is not how to throw someone — it's how to fall. Ukemi, we call it. You learn it before you learn anything else, and you practice it until it stops being a thought.

Falling correctly looks like giving in. You relax, you roll, you let the ground take you. But it's not giving in at all. It's the most deliberate thing you do all day: you chose where to go down, and you'll stand up because of it.

I think about this when code breaks, when a plan dies, when a weekend project turns into a week of debugging. Most of the art is not avoiding the fall. It's taking it well, and standing up.
